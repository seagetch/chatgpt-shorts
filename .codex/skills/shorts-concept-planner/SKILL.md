# Shorts Concept Planner / 全作品共通企画

## Responsibility

Choose **what story to tell**, not how a genre specialist will solve its mechanics. Use for all new shorts across series and standalone works, before any genre-specific writer. A user-specified genre, series, or premise is a hard constraint: diversify within that scope, never override it to satisfy a rotation. For a narrow revision that preserves the premise, run only the relevant consistency/repetition checks.

Keep `idea.md` as the concept, comparison, and decision record and `doc.md` as the prose source of truth. Never create a new work by copying the previous episode's outline or changing only its setting.

## Multi-axis genre map: prompts, not quotas

Broaden candidate creation by considering these families, including subgenres and hybrids:

1. SF / speculative: hard, soft, space, first contact, time, parallel worlds, dystopia, AI, cyberpunk, biotech, social SF, everyday SF.
2. Mystery / deduction: fair-play, impossible crime, inverted, everyday puzzle, police, legal, historical, psychological, social, cozy.
3. Suspense / thriller: pursuit, hostage, espionage, political, corporate, techno, medical, conspiracy, survival.
4. Horror / uncanny: ghost, folklore, urban legend, cosmic, body, psychological, monster, technological, quotidian intrusion, absurd.
5. Fantasy: epic, low, urban, **異世界転生・異世界転移・異世界召喚・異世界生活・異世界職業もの・異世界スローライフ・異世界コメディ**, **悪役令嬢（破滅回避・婚約破棄・断罪・領地経営・復讐・恋愛・逆転劇）**, sword and sorcery, myth, folktale, magical realism.
6. Romance / relationships: comedy, first love, adulthood, separation, reunion, marriage, friendship, family, queer relationships.
7. Coming of age: school, club, exam, craft, apprenticeship, talent, failure, self-determination.
8. Comedy / humor: **小噺・一席もの・落語的な会話劇・頓智・洒落・勘違い・オチの効いた掌編**, daily, dialogue, farce, mistaken identity, slapstick, black comedy, social satire, parody, nonsense.
9. Historical / period: historical drama, swordplay, war, court, alternate history, folk life, legend.
10. Adventure / action: exploration, treasure hunt, sea, air, espionage, martial arts, heist, escape, buddy action.
11. Social / political / economic: society, elections, bureaucracy, corporate, finance, labor, media, justice, inequality, ecology.
12. Workplace / vocation: healthcare, science, engineering, craft, hospitality, food, publishing, education, transport, emergency, farming.
13. Sport / competition: athletics, combat, motor, e-sports, mind games, performing competition, invented sport.
14. Everyday / literary: family, interior life, travel, aging, loss, memory, solitude, quiet change, experimental literary fiction.
15. Children / fable: animal, adventure, fairy tale, nonsense, moral fable.
16. Religion / philosophy: existence, ethics, gods, death, identity, free will, perception.
17. Disaster / extreme: natural disaster, accident, rescue, epidemic, war at home, confined space, resource scarcity.
18. Formal experiments: epistolary, diary, report, dialogue-only, monologue, multiple testimonies, reverse order, loop, nested, unreliable narration, metafiction, single-scene.
19. **ライトノベル／キャラクター小説**: 学園ラノベ、異能バトル、冒険ファンタジー、ラブコメ、日常系、キャラクター掛け合い、バディ、転生・追放・成り上がり、Web小説的な強い導入と爽快な転換、コメディ主体の短編。ライトノベルは文体・読者期待・市場形式でもあり、他の主ジャンルと組み合わせて指定する。
20. **異世界・悪役令嬢系（独立した企画枠としても検討）**: 転生先での生活、ゲーム世界、婚約破棄、断罪回避、破滅フラグ、宮廷陰謀、領地・経営、貴族社会のコメディ、主従逆転、複数視点の誤解、恋愛以外の解決。既存のテンプレ展開を安易に反復せず、人物の能動的選択と短編の独立した決着を作る。

21. **異世界転生（異世界転移とは独立した主ジャンル）**: 前世の記憶を持って別人・別種族・別の地位に生まれ変わる／死後の再出発／幼少期からのやり直し／転生先での成長／転生後の家族・社会との関係／前世の技能の限界と誤用／転生特典・チート能力の代償／転生先での職業・領地経営・冒険・恋愛・コメディ・復讐／転生そのものを疑う変化球。**異世界転移**（元の身体のまま別世界へ移動）や**現代への転生**とは区別して明示的に選択・記録する。短編では転生を単なる導入説明にせず、転生によって生まれた選択・対立・変化を本編の核に置く。

### Isekai reincarnation planning check

When selecting 異世界転生, record: (a) who the protagonist was in the prior life and what matters from it; (b) who they become, including species, age, status, limitations and relationships; (c) whether prior memories are complete or partial, and what abilities actually carry over; (d) what the new world's independently consistent social and magical rules are; (e) what they can choose **now** that makes this episode a story rather than a setup; (f) how the result fits the intended short length. Avoid automatic invincibility, generic skill menus, obligatory truck accidents, and unearned instant admiration unless the story intentionally uses or parodies those conventions. Distinguish reincarnation from summoning and body-preserving transfer in the concept and reader-facing label.

**Short-form Japanese literary modes must not be erased by a broad Western genre label.** For each idea explicitly consider whether 小噺、掌編コメディ、ライトノベル、異世界転生・転移、悪役令嬢、Web小説風の短編 are viable creative modes alongside literary, SF, detective, or dramatic works. They must be allowed as the PRIMARY intended reader experience, not merely subordinated as a minor flavor. Avoid always choosing restrained realism over playful/genre-forward premises.

Genres are **not** plot engines. Independently vary: puzzle, reversal, duel, pursuit/escape, quest/discovery, growth, relationship shift, sacrifice/choice, fall/tragedy, escalating farce, competition, revenge, cumulative repetition, observation, allegory/satire. Choose an engine that creates concrete choices and change, not just an atmospheric label.

## Mandatory planning order

### 1. Capture constraints and continuity

Write fixed user requirements, series continuity, intended length, audience, format and exclusions. For a series, read character/world canon **without reading old plot summaries first**. For standalone work, do not imply series obligations.

### Default length and form decision (record before plotting)

Unless the user specifies a different length, set the working target to **3,000–4,000 Japanese characters (excluding whitespace)**, with **2,500–5,000** as a flexible genre/structure-dependent neighborhood. Record the target and the narrative tasks it must accommodate in `idea.md`; do not make a 1,000–1,500-character summary the unannounced default. A work planned below **2,000 characters** requires an intentional form (such as 小噺・落語的一席, 寓話, flash fiction), a concrete explanation of why the dramatic setup, escalation and payoff fully fit, and independent review under the orchestrator's length gate. Never add filler solely to hit the range. User-specified length overrides the default; the chosen form still must function as a complete story.

### 2. External discovery and research — before genre selection

For each new premise, actively research **current public material** before deciding the genre and candidate engines. Use available live web/news research tools (and primary sources when appropriate); do not rely solely on model memory or existing chatgpt-shorts stories.

Scan across **different time scales and depths**, not only whatever is trending today:
- **Current events and public conversation:** recent domestic and international news, emerging controversies, social changes, public debates, notable events, cultural phenomena, and what ordinary people are discussing.
- **Trends and audience interests:** contemporary fiction, film, anime, games, online communities, reading trends, genre conventions being challenged, and overlooked interests. Social-media volume is not evidence of universal public interest.
- **Deep and evergreen material:** science, technology, history, archaeology, anthropology, philosophy, law, economics, ecology, religion, labor, unfamiliar professions, regional folklore, and long-running structural tensions; look for surprising facts and unresolved questions rather than just headlines.
- **Contrasting perspectives:** specialist commentary, official reports, original research, affected people, and credible disagreement, to reveal interesting human stakes instead of a shallow headline summary.

Research multiple **unrelated** topic clusters before choosing the genre. Prefer at least **six distinct leads spanning three domains**, with a mixture of current and deep/evergreen material when the brief allows. Make the search broad enough to avoid an SF-only, mystery-only, political-only or disaster-only drift. Record **the date searched**, source title, URL, publication/event date when available, the grounded factual observation, uncertainty, and the fictional dramatic question it suggests. Separate published facts, interpretations, and speculative extrapolations.

Translate research into **original story mechanisms**: who wants what, which values collide, what surprising constraint changes someone's options, what concrete decision follows, and which genre best dramatizes it. The source topic must not automatically determine genre: a scientific story may inspire romance or comedy; a political incident may inspire domestic drama; folklore may inspire mystery rather than horror. Consider research-derived ideas alongside internally invented ones; do not equate topicality with merit.

**Ethical and editorial safeguards:** Do not closely fictionalize a recent real victim's suffering, misrepresent allegations as facts, imply invented wrongdoing by identifiable real people, or copy the plot/characters/expression of existing works. Favor transformation, synthesis, changed settings/characters, and original causal design. Check contested and time-sensitive claims against credible dated sources; state uncertainty rather than invent verification. Avoid fashionable jargon and exposition dumping into prose. An enduring human conflict can outweigh a fleeting trend.

If live research cannot be performed, mark `EXTERNAL RESEARCH: NOT VERIFIED` with the reason and proceed transparently using reliable known material and independent imagination; do not falsely claim the trends were checked. If the user explicitly prohibits web research, respect that constraint.

### 3. Independent divergence

After external discovery, and before looking at existing plots, generate **at least six substantively different candidate concepts** unless the user asks for one predetermined exact plot. When the user fixes the genre, ensure six different mechanisms within it rather than six genre labels. Consider differing primary genre, engine, stakes, setting/social sphere, protagonist role, viewpoint, narrative pace, tone, and ending. At least four axes must vary across the candidate pool.

For each concept capture:
- one-sentence hook and central conflict;
- protagonist's want, pressure and irreversible choice;
- primary genre plus engine (independently labeled);
- distinctive midpoint/climax and ending consequence;
- why the idea works at short-story length.

Reject cosmetic substitutions (new object / different hall / new job but identical incident and resolution).

### 4. Negative-use archive audit

**Only after independent candidates exist**, inspect actual recent `doc.md` and `idea.md` plus older related or same-series works. Previous stories are for **detecting repeated structures and verifying canon**, never source prompts. Do not read only titles and pretend structural comparison is complete.

Compare genre, premise, conflict, driving question, mechanism, character roles, scene order, location, tone, climax and final image. Look for clusters: repeated SF apocalypse, mild object mysteries, passive first-person realization, quiet reconciliations, repeated terminal one-liners, or repeated supernatural rule reveals. A change in prose decoration alone does not count.

Record which works were checked and classify candidates as distinct / adjacent / repetitive, with concrete matches. If the archive is unavailable mark NOT VERIFIED; don't claim originality as established.

### 5. Select by quality, then diversity

Score remaining candidates 0–5 each on: drama and emotional stakes; true novelty vs archive; coherent action/causal potential; distinctive short-form shape; satisfying payoff. Consider research depth and relevance as supporting evidence, not as a substitute for story quality or as a reward for merely mentioning news. Note risks and choose for reader impact, not rigid quota. A strong return to a familiar genre is acceptable when its *mechanism* differs; an unconvincing story in an unused genre is not.

Before prose, state in one or two sentences what is fundamentally new compared with recent works. If the answer is only a new prop, venue, culprit, or genre badge: FAIL, regenerate.

### 6. Output handoff contract

Record in `idea.md`:
- fixed constraints and canon;
- dated external discovery log with diverse news/trend/deep-topic leads, citations/URLs, factual uncertainty, and original dramatic questions (or explicit NOT VERIFIED status);
- independently generated candidate table, created before archive consultation;
- actual works inspected and similarity comparison;
- selection scores, rejected options, reason for choice;
- selected primary genre, secondary influences, **reader-facing format/market mode (e.g. 小噺／ライトノベル／異世界／悪役令嬢 when applicable)**, plot engine, stakes, tone, perspective, decisive events, climax and intended ending;
- known factual research needs, risky assumptions, and applicable specialist skills;
- plan status: PASS / FAIL / NOT VERIFIED.

Only after this record is complete should the orchestration skill call appropriate specialist(s). Never force mystery six-table design into romance, comedy, literary fiction, or action unless the work actually has a fair-play puzzle.

## Idea quality gate

FAIL and return to independent candidate generation if the selected concept:
- essentially repeats an existing mechanism under new names or props;
- has no concrete change, decision, goal, or pressure;
- is too large for the intended length without an intentional focused slice;
- relies on predictable genre stereotypes alone;
- treats every story as an investigative mystery or quiet moral realization;
- cannot explain how the ending is earned by its chosen engine.

The generator may show a short public planning summary, but never inject metadata, audit language or scorecards into the literary text.
