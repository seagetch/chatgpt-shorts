# 久条麗華シリーズ企画: Reika Series Planner

## Goal and scope

Maintain the recognizable personalities and relationship of 久条麗華 and 冬月澄乃 **without** making every episode the same polite, nonfatal, object-based mystery. This is an **upstream planning skill**, separate from mystery mechanics, horror mechanics, prose revision, and publication. Use it for all new series episodes even when the episode is not a conventional mystery. For a revision that changes the central plot, re-run the selection and repetition checks.

**Critical distinction: continuity is not imitation.** Prior works and character notes are a canon and negative-comparison resource, NOT a template for generating the next premise.

## Planning sequence — order is mandatory

### 1. Lock only the genuine series constants

Read the established character reference, currently `20260311-ポーは「通すな」と言わない/characters.md`, for stable identities, speech habits, interpersonal history, and established constraints. Do not treat its example scene rhythm as a compulsory plot.

Preserve the personalities without requiring:
- 麗華 always jumps to a false conclusion first;
- 澄乃 always corrects her in dialogue;
- 澄乃 always narrates in first person (unless requested as a binding series convention);
- a culturally prestigious venue, rare art object, exhibition, or paper document;
- a nonviolent case, small clue, concealed item, or final deductive explanation.

The same dispositions can lead to different actions under different stakes. A quiet event, an action climax, a tragedy, a social comedy, a long investigation, a rivalry, or a purely human conflict may all fit. Do not alter core character facts merely to produce variety.

### 2. Diverge *before* consulting previous episode plots

First propose **at least six meaningfully different candidate story engines**. Use broad genre prompts rather than recalling the most recent installments. Vary at least four independent axes across the pool:

- primary genre/subgenre: classic murder mystery, crime suspense, thriller, adventure, action, comedy/farce, social drama, family drama, political/economic intrigue, courtroom, historical puzzle, horror, speculative/SF, romance, heist, survival, etc.;
- scale and stakes: intimate, personal, local, institutional, national; material, legal, emotional, bodily, reputational;
- inciting event / question: disappearance, witnessed event, pursuit, deception, threat, competition, conflicting duties, an apparently ordinary dilemma, impossible crime;
- plot engine: whodunit, howdunit, whydunit, cat-and-mouse, deadline, escape, negotiation, investigation, rivalry, quest, mistaken identity, tragic choice;
- setting and social milieu: ordinary life, travel, school, business, politics, science, countryside, street culture, workplace, private home, disaster, public event;
- tone and outcome: comic, tense, tragic, bittersweet, ironic, adventurous, unsettling; solved, partial, costly, unresolved by design;
- viewpoint, protagonist initiative, team dynamic, and pacing.

**Do not equate variety with changing only the location, prop, occupation, or culprit.** A new artifact in a different hall is not a new story engine. Avoid substituting a fixed genre roulette for judgment: lists are prompts, not quotas or a rigid rotation.

Each candidate gets a one-sentence hook plus the central conflict, character stakes, plot mechanism, and distinctive climax. Reject underdeveloped pitches rather than padding the count with cosmetic variants.

### 3. Only now audit the archive for *negative similarity*

Read the actual story texts (`doc.md`) and relevant `idea.md` notes across the series, not merely titles or summaries; verify the available series bibliography before claiming completeness. Compare candidates to **all discoverable series episodes**, with special attention to recent episodes. Existing work is an anti-pattern checklist, not material to remix into another story.

Record a compact comparison across:
- premise and driving question;
- trigger, antagonist/pressure and method;
- principal clue or mechanism;
- location/social class/industry;
- protagonist and supporting-character functions;
- event sequence and reversal;
- tone, narrative rhythm, and resolution image.

Flag recurring clusters (e.g., exhibition/heritage object + tampered surface + premature declaration + maid's correction + dignified partial settlement) even when no individual detail is copied. Repeated combinations matter more than superficial overlap.

For each serious candidate, label **distinct / adjacent / repetitive** with the concrete analogues. Penalize structural sameness; a candidate with a different costume and identical core plot fails.

If previous texts are temporarily inaccessible, explicitly mark the repetition audit **NOT VERIFIED**, do not invent archive knowledge, and avoid a confident originality claim.

### 4. Choose for quality *and* variety

Compare the surviving candidates using:
- dramatic interest and conflict (0–5);
- genuine difference from previously published series plots (0–5);
- distinctive role for 麗華 and 澄乃 without formulaic dialogue (0–5);
- believable decisions and feasible scene mechanics (0–5);
- promise of a satisfying ending proportionate to the length (0–5).

Record the scores with a one-line reason for each. Never let a numerical score override an obvious structural duplicate or weak concept. Prefer a strong novel engine over a weak pitch chosen solely to fill an unused genre slot. If the user specifies a genre or premise, honor it and diversify within that constraint rather than forcing a different genre.

Before approval, state in plain language: **How is this episode fundamentally unlike the last few?** The answer cannot be only names, props, titles, scenery, or the clue object.

### 5. Pass the plan to the proper specialist

After premise selection:
- Use `mystery-writer` for an actual fair-play mystery; `mystery-reviewer` and `mystery-reviser` for checking and revising its logic.
- Use `horror-writer` where horror is the primary mechanism, including physical/supernatural continuity gates.
- Use `sf-short-writer` if there is a meaningful scientific/speculative world change.
- Use only the appropriate story-design principles for other genres; do not force all episodes into six mystery tables merely because the series has a detective persona.
- Use `shorts-reader-maintainer` only for publication/reader work.

If multiple genres apply, define which story engine is primary and use specialist checks for the relevant mechanism. Do not let specialist skill defaults override the selected series variety.

## Required output artifacts

In the new episode's `idea.md`, **before writing `doc.md`**, record:
1. series constants to preserve;
2. six or more pre-archive independent candidate hooks with axes and engines;
3. archive works checked and a structural comparison of the leading candidates;
4. rejected/selected choices with reasons and quality/diversity scores;
5. selected genre, scale, mechanism, relationship dynamic, tone, and ending plan;
6. handoff to applicable writer/reviewer skills and later verification findings.

This planning trace is production documentation, not text to leak into the story. Do not silently overwrite an existing `idea.md` or retroactively portray later ideas as pre-archive candidates.

## Editorial anti-pattern gate

Mark **FAIL** and return to candidate generation if:
- the same exhibit/ceremony/valuable-object issue merely changes its props;
- a minor discrepancy inevitably leads to a single suspect, then a neat explanation, then a restrained ending without any new dramatic engine;
- 麗華 must blunder and 澄乃 must correct her in the same beats as previous episodes;
- stakes are artificially reduced to keep everything nonfatal and polite;
- series continuity is used as justification to avoid unfamiliar fields, stronger conflict, different pacing, or different endings;
- plot depends on earlier works as prompts rather than original independent invention.

Variety is evaluated over the **sequence of episodes**, not by making any single episode a genre salad. The genre can return later if the actual narrative mechanism differs substantially. A coherent, gripping episode always matters more than checking boxes.
